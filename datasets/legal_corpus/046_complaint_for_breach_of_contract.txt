GENERAL ALLEGATIONS  

6. On or about January 15, 2025, Plaintiff and Defendant entered into a written contract (the “Contract”) whereby Defendant agreed to design, supply, and install a solar power system at Plaintiff's residence located at 1234 Maple Avenue, Seattle, WA.  

7. Under the Contract, Defendant agreed to deliver and install the solar system no later than July 15, 2025.  

8. The Contract expressly required Defendant to:  
   a. Use materials meeting State of Washington building code standards;  
   b. Obtain all necessary permits and inspections;  
   c. Complete the installation by July 15, 2025;  
   d. Provide Plaintiff with a full warranty on all workmanship and materials for a period of five years from the installation date.  

9. Plaintiff paid Defendant a total of $28,500.00 in three installments:  
   a. An initial deposit of $8,500.00 on January 20, 2025;  
   b. A progress payment of $10,000.00 on May 1, 2025;  
   c. A final payment of $10,000.00 on August 1, 2025, following partial completion of the installation.  

10. Plaintiff performed all obligations required under the Contract, including timely payments and providing access to the property for the installation.  

11. Defendant commenced work but failed to complete the installation by the contractual deadline of July 15, 2025. Despite assurances that the installation would be completed by August 30, 2025, Defendant did not complete work until November 15, 2025, more than four months late.  

12. More significantly, the installed system was defective in multiple respects including:  
   a. Use of non-certified solar panels that failed to meet industry standards;  
   b. Faulty wiring causing intermittent power outages;  
   c. Failure to obtain or pass final inspection from the Seattle Department of Construction and Inspections, rendering the system non-operational.  

13. Plaintiff notified Defendant in writing on December 1, 2025, of the defects and demanded immediate repair or replacement.  

14. Defendant failed and refused to adequately repair the defects or bring the installation into compliance with the Contract.  

15. Due to Defendant’s breach, Plaintiff has suffered damages including, but not limited to, additional costs of $12,400.00 incurred to hire a third-party contractor to repair and complete the installation as well as loss of anticipated utility cost savings and diminution in property value estimated at $7,500.00.
