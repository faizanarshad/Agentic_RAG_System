MOTION TO DISMISS

I. INTRODUCTION

1. Nova Tech Solutions Ltd. (“Nova Tech”) brings this motion to dismiss the claim brought by Golden Sky Energy Inc. (“Golden Sky”) pursuant to Rule 21.01(1)(a) of the Ontario Rules of Civil Procedure on the basis that the action is statute-barred under the Limitations Act, 2002, S.O. 2002, c. 24, Sch. B (“Limitations Act”).

2. The Plaintiff’s claim arises from an alleged breach of contract regarding a software licensing agreement dated June 15, 2022, between the parties (the “Agreement”).

3. Golden Sky commenced this action by Statement of Claim issued on February 1, 2027—more than four years after the alleged breach on January 31, 2023.

4. The Limitations Act prescribes a two-year basic limitation period for contract claims, which bars claims brought after two years from the date on which the claim was discovered, or ought reasonably to have been discovered.

5. For these reasons, Nova Tech requests that the action be dismissed with costs.

II. FACTUAL BACKGROUND

6. On or about June 15, 2022, Golden Sky and Nova Tech entered into the Agreement, pursuant to which Nova Tech licensed proprietary software solutions to Golden Sky for use in its oil and gas exploration operations.

7. The Agreement required Nova Tech to deliver software updates and maintenance services on a quarterly basis and to ensure software functionality consistent with agreed-upon specifications.

8. According to the Plaintiff, Nova Tech breached the Agreement by failing to provide a critical software update promised for delivery by January 31, 2023.

9. Golden Sky alleges that this breach caused operational disruptions and financial losses amounting to $1,250,000.

10. The Plaintiff did not take any steps to notify Nova Tech of these alleged breaches or seek remedies until a demand letter sent on January 25, 2027.

11. On February 1, 2027, Golden Sky commenced this legal action.

III. ISSUES

12. The sole issue on this Motion is whether the Plaintiff’s claim is statute-barred by the Limitations Act, thereby justifying dismissal under Rule 21.01(1)(a).

IV. LAW AND ARGUMENT

A. The Basic Limitation Period Under the Limitations Act

13. Section 4(1) of the Limitations Act establishes a two-year limitation period for most civil claims, including breach of contract claims: “An action respecting a claim shall not be commenced after the second anniversary of the day on which the claim was discovered.”

14. Section 5(1) defines when a claim is discovered. It states that a claim is discovered on the earlier of the day on which the plaintiff first knew, or ought to have known, the material facts regarding the injury, loss or damage, the identity of the responsible party, and that a proceeding would be an appropriate means to seek remedy.

B. Application of the Limitations Period to the Facts

15. Here, Golden Sky alleges that Nova Tech failed to deliver the critical update by January 31, 2023.

16. As a sophisticated corporate entity, Golden Sky ought reasonably to have discovered the breach on or shortly after January 31, 2023.

17. Golden Sky did not commence proceeding until February 1, 2027, over four years after the alleged breach.

18. No material facts suggest that Golden Sky was unaware of the breach or its effects until a later date.

19. Nor does the Plaintiff claim any applicable exceptions such as fraud, concealment, or disability to extend the limitation period.

20. Thus, the claim is clearly statute-barred because the claim was commenced beyond the two-year limitation period.

21. The Ontario Court of Appeal has held in numerous cases, including XYZ Construction Inc. v. ABC Mechanical Ltd., 2018 ONCA 556, that strict adherence to limitation periods is essential to promote finality and certainty in commercial disputes.

C. No Valid Exception Applies

22. Section 16 of the Limitations Act provides certain discretionary extensions, including where fraud or deliberate concealment is involved.

23. Golden Sky alleges no such misconduct.

24. The Plaintiff’s delay is unexplained and inexcusable.

25. Further, no separate discovery rule or equitable doctrine applies to extend the limitation period beyond two years from breach.

V. CONCLUSION AND ORDER SOUGHT

(a) The action be dismissed with costs pursuant to Rule 21.01(1)(a) of the Ontario Rules of Civil Procedure;

(b) The Court order such further and other relief as this Honourable Court may deem just.
