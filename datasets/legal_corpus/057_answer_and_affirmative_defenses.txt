Introduction  

1. This is the Answer and Affirmative Defences of Novalis Holdings PLC (“Novalis” or “Defendant”) in response to the Claim Form and Particulars of Claim issued by Luxor Enterprises Limited (“Luxor” or “Claimant”) dated 12 January 2027, which alleges fraud and breach of fiduciary duty.  

2. Novalis denies all allegations of wrongdoing and liability and puts the Claimant to strict proof thereof.  

Defined Terms  

3. For the purposes of this Answer, the following defined terms shall apply:  
   (a) “Agreement” means the Shareholders’ Agreement dated 15 February 2025 between Luxor and Novalis;  
   (b) “Board” means the board of directors of Novalis;  
   (c) “Confidential Information” means information designated as confidential or which by its nature ought to be confidential relating to Novalis’ business;  
   (d) “Relevant Period” means the period from 1 March 2025 to 31 December 2026.  

Answer to Particulars of Claim  

4. Novalis admits that Luxor and Novalis entered into the Agreement on 15 February 2025. Novalis admits that the Agreement sets out certain rights and obligations of the parties in respect of their shareholdings in Novalis.  

5. Novalis denies the allegation at paragraph 8 of the Particulars of Claim that Novalis, through its directors or agents, knowingly made false representations to Luxor with the intent to induce Luxor to transfer additional shares to Novalis or to forego certain rights under the Agreement. Novalis states that all communications were bona fide and made in good faith based on available information at the time.  

6. Novalis denies the allegation at paragraph 10 that Novalis intentionally concealed material facts or documents relating to Novalis’ financial position or prospects. Novalis states that all disclosures made to Luxor complied with the Agreement and applicable law.  

7. Novalis denies the allegation at paragraph 12 that Novalis breached any fiduciary duty owed to Luxor. Novalis asserts that the directors acted in accordance with their duties under the Companies Act 2006 and the terms of the Agreement.  

8. Novalis admits that profits for the financial year ending 31 December 2026 were £4.2 million but denies that any profits were improperly withheld from Luxor or that distributions were unlawfully withheld.  

9. Novalis denies that Luxor has suffered any loss or damage attributable to any act or omission of Novalis.  

Affirmative Defences  

10. Without prejudice to the foregoing denials, Novalis asserts the following affirmative defences:  

First Affirmative Defence: Lack of Fraudulent Intent  

11. The Claimant’s claim for fraud fails as Novalis did not act with fraudulent intent. All statements and representations made were true to the best of Novalis’ knowledge and belief at the time they were made. Any errors were made inadvertently and without intention to deceive.  

12. There is no evidence of dishonesty or fraudulent conduct by Novalis or its directors.  

Second Affirmative Defence: No Breach of Fiduciary Duty  

13. The directors of Novalis owed fiduciary duties to the company as a whole under the Companies Act 2006 and common law. These duties were discharged appropriately.  

14. The Claimant was not a direct beneficiary of any fiduciary duty owed by Novalis’ directors, and any duty owed was to the company, not to individual shareholders save to the extent provided by law and the Agreement.  

15. The directors acted in good faith, with due care, and in the interests of Novalis and its shareholders collectively. No breach of fiduciary duty occurred.  

Third Affirmative Defence: Compliance With Agreement and Law  

16. Novalis complied with all express and implied terms of the Agreement, including those concerning disclosure obligations, profit distributions, and shareholder communications.  

17. Novalis acted within the scope of its lawful powers and authority. No breach of contract or fiduciary obligation has arisen.  

Fourth Affirmative Defence: Contributory Negligence and Estoppel  

18. Novalis pleads that if any loss has been suffered by Luxor, which is denied, such loss was caused or contributed to by Luxor’s own failure to exercise due diligence and to comply with procedural obligations under the Agreement.  

19. Luxor had opportunities to request information and to participate in board meetings but failed to do so in a timely and responsible manner.  

20. Luxor is estopped from asserting claims based on information or representations it knew or ought to have known were incomplete or inaccurate.  

Fifth Affirmative Defence: Delay and Laches  

21. By virtue of the Relevant Period and the dates on which events complained of occurred, the Claimant has delayed unreasonably in bringing these claims. This delay has prejudiced Novalis’ ability to respond and to obtain relevant evidence.  

22. The Claimant’s claims are barred or limited by the doctrine of laches or applicable limitation provisions in the Limitation Act 1980.  

Sixth Affirmative Defence: No Causation or Loss  

23. Novalis denies that any act or omission alleged has caused the Claimant to suffer any loss. The Claimant has failed to specify with adequate particularity any loss suffered.  

24. Any alleged loss would have arisen in the ordinary course of business risk and not as a consequence of any wrongful act by Novalis.
