III. Nature of the Matter  
This litigation involves a contractual dispute arising from a supply agreement executed between Smithson Technologies Inc. (“Smithson” or “Plaintiff”), a corporation incorporated under the laws of Ontario with its head office located at 1200 Commerce Way, Toronto, Ontario, and Greenvale Manufacturing Ltd. (“Greenvale” or “Defendant”), an Ontario-based manufacturing company located at 450 Industrial Road, Hamilton, Ontario.  

IV. Background and Chronology of Events  

1. On January 15, 2025, Smithson and Greenvale entered into a Supply Agreement (the “Agreement”) pursuant to which Greenvale was to manufacture and deliver 10,000 units of precision components (the “Products”) to Smithson by no later than September 30, 2025. The total contract price was $2,500,000 CAD, payable in four equal installments.  

2. The Agreement stipulated that Greenvale must maintain quality standards consistent with ISO 9001:2015 certification and provide monthly progress reports to Smithson. Failure to meet delivery deadlines or quality specifications would constitute a material breach.  

3. Smithson paid the first two installments, totaling $1,250,000 CAD, on February 1, 2025, and May 1, 2025, respectively.  

4. Problems arose beginning August 2025 when Greenvale failed to deliver the first batch of 4,000 components, citing machinery breakdowns and supply chain delays for raw materials. Despite multiple extensions, the product did not meet agreed specifications upon inspection.  

5. Smithson issued a formal Notice of Default on October 15, 2025, demanding corrective action and delivery within 30 days. Greenvale responded on October 25, 2025, acknowledging the delays but requesting additional time citing unforeseen circumstances.  

6. On November 30, 2025, Smithson elected to terminate the Agreement for cause, citing repeated failures to meet deadlines and quality standards, and commenced legal proceedings seeking damages in the amount of $1,000,000 CAD for lost profits and costs incurred.  

7. Greenvale filed a Statement of Defence on January 15, 2026, denying material breach and asserting that delays were excusable under the force majeure clause contained in Section 12 of the Agreement. Greenvale counterclaimed for $500,000 CAD, alleging Smithson failed to make the final two payments due to an alleged deficiency in product specifications provided by Smithson resulting in production inefficiencies.  

V. Procedural History  

8. Case Management Conferences took place on February 20, 2026, and June 10, 2026. The parties agreed to exchange all relevant documents by July 15, 2026, and to undertake mediation by August 30, 2026.  

9. Disclosure was completed on July 14, 2026. The mediation held on August 28, 2026, was unsuccessful.

11. On November 10, 2026, the parties reached an agreement in principle to suspend all proceedings to pursue further settlement discussions.
