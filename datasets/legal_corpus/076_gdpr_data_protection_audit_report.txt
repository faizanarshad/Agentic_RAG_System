Compliance Record No.: TX-GDPR-2026-0042

1. INTRODUCTION  
This GDPR Data Protection Audit Report (“Report”) has been prepared following a comprehensive audit of the data protection practices of Horizon Delta Enterprises, LLC (“the Company” or “Horizon Delta”), a Texas-based entity headquartered at 10521 Crescent Valley Drive, Austin, Texas, 78759. The audit was conducted pursuant to the Texas Data Privacy and Security Compliance Act (TX-DPSCA), modeled on the European Union’s General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), and applicable state privacy laws.  

The audit period covered January 1, 2025, through December 31, 2025. The purpose of this Report is to determine the Company’s compliance with applicable data protection obligations under TX-DPSCA and to identify any areas that require corrective action, including the potential imposition of regulatory fines.  

2. DEFINITIONS  
For the purposes of this Report:  
2.1 “Personal Data” means any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person, as defined in TX-DPSCA Section 3.  
2.2 “Data Subject” means an individual whose Personal Data is processed by the Company.  
2.3 “Processing” refers to any operation performed on Personal Data, including collection, storage, use, disclosure, or deletion.  
2.4 “Controller” means the entity which determines the purposes and means of Processing Personal Data – in this case, Horizon Delta Enterprises, LLC.  
2.5 “Processor” means any third-party service provider processing Personal Data on behalf of the Controller.

3. SCOPE AND METHODOLOGY  
3.1 The audit assessed Horizon Delta’s compliance with key data protection principles, including lawfulness, fairness, transparency, data minimization, accuracy, storage limitation, security, and accountability, as defined in TX-DPSCA §12(b).  
3.2 Documentation reviewed included data privacy policies, records of Processing activities, data breach notifications, employee training records, and third-party audit reports.  
3.3 Interviews were conducted with Horizon Delta’s Data Protection Officer (DPO), IT Security Lead, and Privacy Counsel.  
3.4 Onsite inspections of data storage facilities and cybersecurity infrastructure took place between January 15 and January 30, 2026.  
3.5 Data samples were tested across three business units: Customer Services, Marketing, and Human Resources.

4. FINDINGS  

4.1 Data Inventory and Records of Processing Activities  
The Company maintains a partial record of Processing activities. However, documentation lacks sufficient detail concerning third-party Processors engaged for marketing campaigns and customer analytics. Specifically, contract terms fail to outline obligations regarding data security and breach notification within mandated timelines (TX-DPSCA §15).  

4.2 Lawfulness and Transparency  
Horizon Delta collects Personal Data primarily through online platforms and in-person customer interactions. Consent mechanisms were inconsistent; opt-in forms lack clarity on the scope of data use, and in some instances, pre-ticked boxes were used, raising concerns under TX-DPSCA §14 regarding freely given and specific consent. Privacy notices are not prominently displayed on mobile applications.  

4.3 Data Subject Rights  
Procedures exist for handling Data Subject Access Requests (DSARs) but were not executed within the statutory 30-day deadline in two of five sampled cases, violating TX-DPSCA §22(b). There is no formalized process for Data Portability requests or for erasure (“right to be forgotten”).  

4.4 Data Minimization and Storage Limitation  
The Company retains customer data beyond the stated retention period of two years without documented justification, contrary to TX-DPSCA §18(a). Historical marketing databases include records as old as 2014, with no evidence of regular deletion or anonymization.  

4.5 Security of Processing  
Security controls were generally adequate with the implementation of encryption in transit and at rest for sensitive Personal Data. However, an unpatched vulnerability was identified on a public-facing web server (CVE-2025-1897), potentially exposing approximately 12,000 Data Subjects’ data. Incident response protocols were only partially documented and had not been tested in the previous 12 months.  

4.6 Data Breach Notification  
A data breach affecting the marketing database occurred on November 10, 2025. Notification to the Texas Department of Privacy and affected Data Subjects was provided on January 5, 2026, exceeding the 72-hour notification requirement under TX-DPSCA §25(c).  

5. RISK ASSESSMENT  
The combined effect of insufficient Transparency, delayed DSAR responses, extended data retention, and the late breach notification represent systemic compliance weaknesses. These deficiencies expose Horizon Delta to regulatory enforcement actions, including substantial fines, reputational damage, and potential civil claims.  

6. RECOMMENDATIONS AND CORRECTIVE ACTIONS  

6.1 Record of Processing Activities  
The Company must update and complete comprehensive records of all Processing activities, including detailed contracts with Processors, by June 30, 2026.  

6.2 Consent and Transparency  
Revise all consent mechanisms to comply with TX-DPSCA requirements. Remove pre-ticked boxes and prominently display privacy notices on mobile and web platforms. Deadline: August 31, 2026.  

6.3 Data Subject Rights  
Implement formalized procedures for all Data Subject rights, including Data Portability and erasure. Train staff on DSAR handling to ensure compliance with the 30-day response period. Deadline: July 31, 2026.  

6.4 Data Retention and Minimization  
Establish and enforce policies for data retention and regular purging of outdated Personal Data, adhering strictly to retention schedules. Conduct quarterly reviews. Deadline: August 31, 2026.  

6.5 Security and Incident Response  
Patch identified vulnerabilities immediately. Conduct a full security audit and implement a regular penetration testing schedule. Develop and test an incident response plan semi-annually. Deadline for initial remediation: May 15, 2026.  

6.6 Breach Notification Compliance  
Develop protocols to ensure breach notifications are sent within 72 hours of incident discovery, including staff training and notification templates. Deadline: June 30, 2026.  

7. POTENTIAL REGULATORY FINE  
Based on findings, the Texas Data Protection Authority (TDPA) may impose a regulatory fine under TX-DPSCA §41, which authorizes penalties up to 4% of the Company’s global annual turnover or $5 million USD, whichever is greater, for serious infringements. Given the scale of the breach and procedural violations, a proposed administrative fine of $2,300,000 USD is anticipated, subject to appeal.  

Horizon Delta is advised to engage in expedited remediation and consider voluntary disclosure measures to mitigate potential penalties.  

8. CONCLUSION  
Horizon Delta Enterprises, LLC has demonstrated awareness of data protection obligations but currently falls short of full compliance with the Texas Data Privacy and Security Compliance Act. Immediate and sustained corrective actions are necessary to prevent escalation of regulatory risks and to safeguard Data Subjects’ rights and trust. The Company is encouraged to adopt a privacy-by-design approach and prioritize continuous compliance monitoring.
