1. Introduction  

1.1 This GDPR Data Protection Audit Report (“Report”) is issued pursuant to the compliance requirements under the General Data Protection Regulation (EU) (“GDPR”), as applicable within the jurisdiction of the State of Delaware pursuant to Delaware Code Title 6, Chapter 12A, referencing GDPR compliance in entities processing personal data of EU data subjects.  

1.2 This Report assesses the compliance status of Virexa Technologies LLC (“Virexa” or the “Company”), a Delaware limited liability company involved in cloud-based software services, regarding GDPR obligations during the audit period of January 15, 2025 to March 30, 2025.  

1.3 The audit was performed by Compliance Insight Solutions, Inc., an independent data privacy consultancy (“Consultants”), under engagement letter No. CIS-DE-VX-0125 dated January 5, 2025.  

2. Definitions  

2.1 “Personal Data” means any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person as defined under GDPR Article 4(1).  

2.2 “Data Controller” means the natural or legal person that determines the purposes and means of the processing of personal data (GDPR Article 4(7)).  

2.3 “Data Processor” means a natural or legal person processing personal data on behalf of the controller (GDPR Article 4(8)).  

2.4 “Data Subject” means the identified or identifiable individual to whom the personal data relates (GDPR Article 4(1)).  

2.5 “Processing” means any operation performed on personal data as defined in GDPR Article 4(2).  

3. Background and Scope  

3.1 Virexa Technologies LLC, headquartered in Wilmington, Delaware, provides cloud software solutions predominantly to clients in the EU member states, thus subjecting it to GDPR obligations as a Data Processor and Data Controller in certain contexts.  

3.2 The scope of this audit includes:  
  3.2.1 Evaluation of Virexa’s policies, procedures, and technical controls for lawful processing of EU Data Subjects’ personal data.  
  3.2.2 Assessment of data subject rights management, data breach response protocols, and cross-border data transfer mechanisms.  
  3.2.3 Verification of record-keeping and documentation pursuant to Article 30 GDPR.  

3.3 The audit excluded third-party vendor compliance but reviewed vendor risk assessments maintained by Virexa.  

4. Executive Summary  

4.1 The overall compliance posture demonstrates significant efforts by Virexa Technologies LLC to align with GDPR requirements. Documented policies are in place, with designated Data Protection Officer (DPO) Ms. Helena Trowbridge appointed on August 1, 2024.  

4.2 Nonetheless, three critical compliance gaps were identified during the audit that pose material risk to lawful processing and potential enforcement action under GDPR:  

    a. Incomplete records of processing activities (Article 30 GDPR).  
    b. Deficiencies in Data Subject Access Request (DSAR) response procedures and timing.  
    c. Insufficient technical safeguards for cross-border data transfers under Chapter V GDPR.  

4.3 The Company is required to remediate these findings within specified deadlines as set forth below to meet compliance obligations.  

5. Detailed Findings and Recommendations  

5.1 Finding 1: Incomplete Records of Processing Activities  

5.1.1 Observation: The Company’s documented records of processing activities (“RoPA”) were missing descriptions for three major processing operations relating to marketing data and customer profiling. This non-compliance contravenes Article 30(1)(a) through (d) GDPR which mandates comprehensive records detailing processing purposes, categories of data subjects and recipients, and retention periods.  

5.1.2 Risk: Failure to maintain complete RoPA undermines accountability principles and complicates supervisory authority inspections under GDPR, exposing Virexa to enforcement actions including fines up to €10 million or 2% of annual worldwide turnover pursuant to GDPR Article 83(4).  

5.1.3 Recommendation: Virexa must update and complete all processing records within 60 calendar days from issuance of this Report, i.e., by June 14, 2025. The updated RoPA should be reviewed and certified by the DPO.  

5.2 Finding 2: Deficient Data Subject Access Request (DSAR) Handling  

5.2.1 Observation: Analysis of DSAR logs showed that 3 of 15 sampled requests during Q1 2025 were not fulfilled within the mandatory one-month timeframe as stipulated under GDPR Article 12(3). Additionally, communication templates lack clarity on data subject rights and appeal options.  

5.2.2 Risk: Delayed or inadequate DSAR responses infringe data subjects’ rights under GDPR and risk complaints to supervisory authorities, reputational damage, and potential sanctions under GDPR Articles 12 and 83.  

5.2.3 Recommendation: Virexa is required to:  
(a) Revise and implement updated DSAR processing workflows to ensure requests are responded to within 30 days.  
(b) Enhance communication templates to include explicit reference to data subjects’ rights and complaint mechanisms.  
These measures shall be completed and evidenced by a follow-up report to Compliance Insight Solutions no later than July 15, 2025.  

5.3 Finding 3: Insufficient Technical Safeguards for Cross-Border Data Transfers  

5.3.1 Observation: The Company transfers personal data collected from EU data subjects to its U.S.-based data centers. While Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs) are contractually incorporated, there is inadequate technical encryption and pseudonymization of transferred data as recommended under the European Data Protection Board guidance (EDPB/2023/03).  

5.3.2 Risk: Insufficient technical safeguards may expose personal data to unauthorized access during transfer, violating GDPR Chapter V compliance and inviting investigation or restrictions by Delaware or EU supervisory bodies.  

5.3.3 Recommendation:  
Virexa must implement enhanced encryption protocols (AES-256 or equivalent) and pseudonymization measures for all personal data transfers outside the EEA within 90 days (by July 14, 2025). Confirmation of implementation and results of internal penetration testing must be provided by July 30, 2025.  

6. Additional Observations  

6.1 Data Breach Notification Procedures: The Company has a documented incident response plan compliant with GDPR Article 33. However, internal training refreshers are recommended at six-month intervals to maintain staff awareness.  

6.2 Vendor Management: Vendor risk assessments are maintained but not regularly updated. Annual reviews scheduled for Q4 2025 are advised to ensure ongoing compliance.
