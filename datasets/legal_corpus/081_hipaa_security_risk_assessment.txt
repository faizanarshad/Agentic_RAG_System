1. Introduction and Purpose  
This HIPAA Security Risk Assessment (“Assessment”) has been conducted in accordance with the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act of 1996 (“HIPAA”) Security Rule requirements at 45 CFR Part 164, Subpart C, applicable to Meridian Health Systems, Inc. (“Meridian” or “Covered Entity”), a California-based healthcare provider. The purpose of this Assessment is to identify potential security risks and vulnerabilities to the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of electronic Protected Health Information (“ePHI”) maintained by Meridian, and to evaluate the effectiveness of administrative, physical, and technical safeguards in place as of the date of this report.  

This Assessment specifically addresses vendor-related risks, including third-party service provider security gaps, which have been identified as a critical area for remediation.

---

2. Scope of the Assessment  
The scope of this Assessment includes all electronic systems, network environments, physical locations, personnel, policies, and business associates involved in the creation, transmission, storage, or processing of ePHI at Meridian’s main campus located at 4524 Silver Creek Drive, Sacramento, California, and Meridian’s cloud environments hosting patient records.  

Vendor relationships evaluated include Meridian’s contracted Electronic Health Records (“EHR”) system provider, CareBridge Solutions LLC (“CareBridge”), and its cloud services subcontractor, NexaCloud Technologies (“NexaCloud”).

4. Assessment Methodology  
CalWest Compliance Consulting utilized the National Institute of Standards and Technology (“NIST”) Special Publication 800-66 Revision 1 as the primary framework for this Assessment. The process involved:  

4.1 Documentation Review – Policies, procedures, contracts, and prior audits related to information security and privacy.  
4.2 Interviews – With Meridian’s Compliance Officer, IT Security Manager, and Vendor Oversight Administrator.  
4.3 Technical Testing – Vulnerability scanning, penetration tests, and configuration audits on Meridian’s systems and vendor interfaces.  
4.4 Risk Analysis – Identification and evaluation of threats and vulnerabilities impacting confidentiality, integrity, and availability of ePHI.

5. Definitions  
“ePHI” means electronic Protected Health Information as defined under HIPAA.  
“Vendor-Risk Gap” refers to identified deficiencies in managing security risks introduced by third-party vendors or subcontractors.  
“Business Associate” has the meaning ascribed under HIPAA and refers to entities, such as CareBridge and NexaCloud, that perform services involving ePHI on behalf of Meridian.

6. Summary of Findings  

6.1 Administrative Safeguards  
Meridian maintains a comprehensive Information Security Policy last updated January 15, 2025. Staff training on HIPAA security takes place biannually with documented attendance. Access controls to ePHI systems include role-based permissions and unique user IDs. However, contract review processes for vendors require improvement to ensure consistent security requirements are enforced.  

6.2 Physical Safeguards  
Physical access controls at Meridian’s Sacramento facility include badge access and surveillance cameras operating 24/7. Backup media are stored securely in locked safes with environmental controls.  

6.3 Technical Safeguards  
Encryption of ePHI is enforced both in transit and at rest using AES-256 standards. Multi-factor authentication (MFA) is enabled for all remote system access. Regular vulnerability scans are performed quarterly.  

6.4 Vendor Risk Assessment and Gap  
The primary vendor-risk gap identified involves Meridian’s subcontractor, NexaCloud, which hosts Meridian’s cloud-based EHR data. Due to the layered subcontracting structure initiated by CareBridge, Meridian lacks direct contractual agreements with NexaCloud and relies solely on CareBridge’s assurances.  

Critical gaps identified include:  

- Absence of a direct Business Associate Agreement (“BAA”) between Meridian and NexaCloud.  
- Lack of documented oversight or audit rights over NexaCloud’s information security controls.  
- Limited visibility into NexaCloud’s incident response procedures and risk management framework.  

This vendor-risk gap presents a significant compliance exposure under HIPAA, which requires Covered Entities to ensure that all Business Associates implement appropriate safeguards.  

---

7. Risk Assessment Results and Risk Levels  

7.1 Risk: Vendor-Risk Gap with NexaCloud  
Likelihood: High  
Impact: High  
Risk Level: Critical  

The inability to directly manage or verify NexaCloud’s compliance with HIPAA security obligations creates a substantial risk of unauthorized access, data breaches, or service disruptions impacting ePHI.  

7.2 Other Risks Identified  
- Outdated system patches on five Meridian workstations (Medium Risk) – mitigated immediately during assessment.  
- Inadequate logging retention periods in some legacy systems (Low Risk).  

---

8. Recommendations and Action Plan  

8.1 Immediate Actions (Due by September 1, 2025)  
- Meridian must require CareBridge to facilitate a tri-party agreement involving a direct BAA between Meridian and NexaCloud or ensure that CareBridge obtains equivalent assurances and audit rights over NexaCloud, enforceable by Meridian.  
- Meridian’s Vendor Oversight Administrator to implement a quarterly vendor security review schedule for CareBridge and its subcontractors.  
- Patch remediation on all workstations to be completed by August 1, 2025 (completed during assessment).  

8.2 Medium-Term Measures (Due by December 31, 2025)  
- Conduct a formal security audit of CareBridge and NexaCloud’s controls focusing on ePHI confidentiality and integrity.  
- Update Meridian’s Vendor Management Policy to require direct audit rights for all cloud service providers handling ePHI.  
- Enhance incident response coordination protocols between Meridian, CareBridge, and NexaCloud.  

8.3 Long-Term Strategic Improvements (Due by June 30, 2026)  
- Invest in a vendor risk management software platform to automate ongoing compliance monitoring.  
- Provide additional staff training specifically addressing vendor-related risks and contractual compliance.
