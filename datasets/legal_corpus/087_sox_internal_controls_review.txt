1. Introduction

1.1 Purpose  
This document constitutes the official SOX Internal Controls Review (the “Review”) for Grosvenor Technologies, Inc. (“Grosvenor” or the “Company”) in accordance with the requirements of the Sarbanes-Oxley Act of 2002 (SOX), as implemented under the laws of the State of Illinois. This Review assesses the effectiveness of the Company’s internal controls over financial reporting (“ICFR”) for the fiscal year ended December 31, 2024, with particular focus on compliance with Sections 302 and 404 of SOX.

1.2 Scope  
The scope of this Review encompasses all material financial processes, internal control environments, information technology general controls (ITGC), and control design and operation within Grosvenor’s accounting and reporting systems. This includes income statement accounts, balance sheet accounts, and disclosure controls related to the Company’s primary revenue generating units and corporate headquarters located in Chicago, Illinois.

1.3 Definitions  
- “Internal Controls” – Procedures and policies implemented to provide reasonable assurance regarding the reliability of financial reporting and compliance with applicable laws.  
- “Control Deficiency” – A weakness in internal controls that could adversely affect the Company’s ability to initiate, authorize, record, process, or report financial data reliably.  
- “Material Weakness” – A significant deficiency or combination of deficiencies that create a reasonable possibility that a material misstatement of the financial statements will not be prevented or detected on a timely basis.

4. Detailed Findings

4.1 IT Access Controls

4.1.1 Background  
Inadequate IT access controls may lead to unauthorized transactions or data manipulation impacting financial reporting reliability.

4.1.2 Observation  
Audit tests revealed that 4 out of 25 sampled user accounts in the financial reporting system possessed inappropriate elevated privileges not aligned with their job responsibilities. There is also a lack of formal periodic review of user access.

4.1.3 Risk Assessment  
This deficiency could allow unauthorized changes to financial data and violates requirements under Section 404(b) of SOX regarding ITGC.

4.1.4 Recommended Remediation  
- Immediate revocation of excessive privileges by September 30, 2025.  
- Implementation of a formal quarterly access review process by December 31, 2025.

4.1.5 Compliance Deadline  
In line with IDFPR directives, remediation must be completed no later than March 31, 2026, to avoid sanctions.

4.2 Segregation of Duties in Payroll Processing

4.2.1 Background  
Effective segregation of duties is essential to prevent fraudulent payroll disbursements.

4.2.2 Observation  
Payroll clerk duties currently overlap with vendor maintenance functions due to staffing shortages. This represents a control deficiency.

4.2.3 Risk Assessment  
Such overlap increases the risk of fraudulent payroll payments or unauthorized changes to employee data, potentially affecting expense account accuracy.

4.2.4 Recommended Remediation  
- Reassignment of vendor maintenance duties away from payroll clerks by October 31, 2025.  
- Cross-training other finance staff to provide proper segregation.

4.3 Other Observations

- Timeliness of financial close process improved from 12 days in 2023 to 9 days in 2024.  
- Documentation of control activities is comprehensive but would benefit from digital archiving for ease of review.
