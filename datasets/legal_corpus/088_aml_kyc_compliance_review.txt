**I. INTRODUCTION**  

1. This AML/KYC Compliance Review Report (“Report”) results from an on-site examination conducted by the New York Financial Integrity Authority (“NYFIA”) at the offices of Orion Capital Holdings LLC (“Orion” or “the Company”) between January 20 and February 5, 2025.  

2. The scope of the examination included a comprehensive evaluation of Orion’s adherence to anti-money laundering (“AML”) and know your customer (“KYC”) regulatory obligations as mandated under New York State Banking Law Article VIII, and related federal regulations, including but not limited to the Bank Secrecy Act (31 U.S.C. § 5311 et seq.).  

3. This Report identifies critical findings discovered during the examination, sets forth required remedial actions, and establishes deadlines for compliance correction pursuant to NYFIA authority under New York Executive Law § 94-b.  

**II. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY**  

4. Orion Capital Holdings LLC, incorporated in New York in 2018, is a private equity fund manager offering investment advisory and capital management services to institutional and accredited investors.  

5. The examination revealed significant compliance deficiencies in Orion’s AML/KYC procedures and controls. Three critical findings have been identified: (i) inadequate customer due diligence documentation; (ii) failure to maintain timely suspicious activity reporting; and (iii) insufficient risk-based customer monitoring protocols.  

6. Orion is required to implement corrective actions within specified time frames to avoid enforcement actions, including potential civil penalties or license suspension.  

**III. DEFINITIONS**  

7. For the purposes of this Report, the following defined terms shall apply:  

a. “AML” means Anti-Money Laundering, referring to laws, regulations, and procedures intended to prevent criminals from disguising illegally obtained funds as legitimate income.  

b. “KYC” means Know Your Customer, the process of a business verifying the identity, suitability, and risks involved with maintaining a business relationship.  

c. “SAR” means Suspicious Activity Report, a document filed by financial institutions with the Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN) when suspicious transactions or activities are detected.  

d. “CIP” means Customer Identification Program, a process required under federal regulations to verify the identity of customers.  

**IV. FINDINGS AND RECOMMENDATIONS**  

**Finding 1: Inadequate Customer Due Diligence Documentation**  

8. During record review, it was found that Orion’s customer files lacked sufficient documentation to meet regulatory standards. Specifically, 27% of sampled client files (total sample size 150) were missing critical identification documents or did not have updated KYC information in accordance with the firm’s own policies.  

9. This deficiency contravenes New York Banking Law § 595-a, which requires firms to maintain robust CIP and KYC records to prevent the onboarding of illicit actors. Inadequate documentation heightens the risk of money laundering activities going undetected.  

10. Furthermore, the Company’s written AML Policy Manual, last updated in 2021, does not reflect recent regulatory changes enacted in 2024, including enhanced due diligence for politically exposed persons (PEPs).  

**Remediation Requirement:**  
a. Orion shall conduct a full review and update of all client files to ensure compliance with CIP and KYC documentation requirements.  
b. Update the AML Policy Manual to incorporate 2024 regulatory amendments, including specific protocols for PEPs and high-risk clients.  
c. Submit a detailed remediation plan and status report to NYFIA by no later than July 1, 2025.  

**Finding 2: Failure to File Suspicious Activity Reports (SARs) Timely**  

11. Examination findings revealed multiple instances where suspicious transactions detected by Orion were not promptly reported to FinCEN as required under 31 C.F.R. § 1023.320.  

12. Specifically, three separate transactions between May 2024 and December 2024, each exceeding $100,000 and exhibiting red flags such as sudden account activity spikes and transactions involving high-risk jurisdictions, were only reported after a delay exceeding 90 days from detection.  

13. Such delays undermine the effectiveness of the U.S. AML regime and contravene both state and federal regulatory standards imposing a 30-day filing deadline upon determination of suspicion.  

**Remediation Requirement:**  
a. Immediately implement a revised SAR reporting procedure with clear escalation protocols ensuring all suspicious transactions are reported within 30 days.  
b. Provide all compliance staff with refresher training on SAR obligations and timelines by May 15, 2025.  
c. Submit evidence of SAR system improvements and training completion to NYFIA by June 1, 2025.  

**Finding 3: Insufficient Risk-Based Customer Monitoring Protocols**  

14. The firm’s ongoing monitoring program fails to adequately risk-rank customers or adjust monitoring frequency based on risk profile. The examination found that clients categorized as “high risk” were subject to the same level of transaction scrutiny as “low risk” clients, contrary to best practices and regulatory guidance under FinCEN Advisory FIN-2023-A001.  

15. Additionally, the Company’s automated transaction monitoring software had outdated parameters and thresholds that failed to flag several complex transaction patterns identified during the review.  

**Remediation Requirement:**  
a. Conduct a comprehensive risk assessment of the entire client base and implement a tiered monitoring system in accordance with risk profiles.  
b. Upgrade transaction monitoring systems with updated algorithms and thresholds reflecting current money laundering typologies by August 1, 2025.  
c. Provide a post-implementation report on the enhanced monitoring framework to NYFIA by September 15, 2025.
