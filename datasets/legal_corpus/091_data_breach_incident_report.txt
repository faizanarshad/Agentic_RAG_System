1. Introduction  
This Data Breach Incident Report ("Report") has been prepared pursuant to the requirements under the Data Protection Act 2018 and the UK GDPR, governing data processing and breach notification in England and Wales. This Report concerns the security incident experienced by Northfield Solutions Ltd ("NSL" or "the Company"), a private limited company incorporated and operating in England, headquartered at 47 Bramble Street, Manchester M14 5QJ.  

2. Background  
2.1 On 22 January 2026, NSL detected unauthorised access to its internal customer database through abnormal network activity logged by its Security Operations Centre (SOC).  
2.2 The breach was investigated and confirmed by the appointed Data Protection Officer ("DPO"), Ms. Harriet Wells.  
2.3 The breach involved the unauthorised extraction of personal data relating to approximately 35,000 customers, including names, addresses, dates of birth, and partial payment card information.  
2.4 This disclosure triggered NSL’s obligations under the UK GDPR to notify both the Information Commissioner’s Office ("ICO") and the individuals affected.  

3. Incident Description  
3.1 Attack Vector: On 20 January 2026, an advanced persistent threat exploited a zero-day vulnerability in NSL’s legacy database management software, enabling remote code execution and data exfiltration.  
3.2 Duration: The breach persisted undetected for 48 hours, from 20 January 2026 08:00 GMT to 22 January 2026 08:00 GMT.  
3.3 Impacted Data Sets: Customers’ personal data as defined at 2.3 above. Notably, full payment card numbers and CVVs were not compromised as these were stored and processed by a third-party PCI DSS compliant payment processor, SecurePay Ltd.  
3.4 Detection: SOC alarms were triggered by unusual data transfer volumes on 22 January 2026 at 08:05 GMT, prompting emergency incident response procedures.  

4. Legal and Regulatory Context  
4.1 Applicable Law: Data Protection Act 2018 and UK GDPR applied across England and Wales.  
4.2 Notification: The ICO was formally notified within 72 hours of breach detection, as required by Article 33 of the UK GDPR.  
4.3 Risk to Data Subjects: The risk assessment concluded a high likelihood of identity theft and phishing attacks targeting affected customers.  

5. Investigation Findings  
5.1 Finding 1: Inadequate Patch Management  
NSL’s legacy database software had not been patched for a critical vulnerability disclosed publicly on 15 October 2025. The internal IT team failed to apply the vendor’s security updates despite receiving alerts. This gap in vulnerability management directly enabled the breach.  
5.2 Finding 2: Insufficient Network Segmentation  
The network architecture allowed the compromised user credentials to access the entire customer database without restriction. There was a lack of adequate segmentation or use of zero-trust principles, contrary to best security practices.  
5.3 Finding 3: Delayed Incident Response  
Despite detection within minutes by SOC, escalation to senior management and activation of the breach response team was delayed by 5 hours due to unclear internal communication protocols. This delay increased the exposure window.  

6. Remediation Required and Deadlines  
6.1 Remediation 1: Patch Management Policy Overhaul  
NSL must implement a comprehensive patch management policy, including automated vulnerability scanning and enforcement of mandatory patch application within 7 days of release. Compliance proof must be submitted to the ICO no later than 30 June 2026.  
6.2 Remediation 2: Network Segmentation and Access Controls  
NSL is required to redesign its network infrastructure to enforce strict segmentation and employ zero-trust access models for sensitive data systems. Completion and independent third-party validation must be reported by 31 August 2026.  
6.3 Remediation 3: Incident Response Enhancement  
NSL shall revise its incident response plan to establish clear escalation paths, communication protocols, and conduct mandatory quarterly breach response drills with management involvement. The updated plan and drill reports must be submitted by 30 September 2026.  

7. Data Subject Notification and Support  
7.1 Customers affected by the breach were notified by email by 1 February 2026, with information on the nature of the breach, data involved, and recommendations to monitor accounts and report suspicious activity.  
7.2 NSL established a dedicated helpline and offered free credit monitoring services for twelve months to all impacted individuals.  

8. Financial Impact and Liability  
8.1 Direct costs incurred by NSL include forensic investigation fees (£95,000), legal counsel and compliance consulting (£42,500), customer notification and credit monitoring (£78,000), and incident response team overtime (£25,000), totaling £240,500 to date.  
8.2 Potential ICO fines have not yet been determined but may reach up to £10 million or 4% of global annual turnover under Article 83(5) of UK GDPR, subject to outcome of ongoing regulatory review.  
8.3 NSL’s cyber insurance policy may mitigate some financial impact; claim processing is underway.  

9. Litigation and Dispute Status  
9.1 No lawsuits have been filed at this time. NSL’s legal team is monitoring any potential class actions or regulatory enforcement proceedings.  
9.2 The Company is proactively cooperating with the ICO and other authorities.
